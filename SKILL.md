# CV & Cover-Letter Tailoring Guardrails

This skill is the canonical reference for tailoring Bobby's job-application
documents.

Two things are non-negotiable and override everything else in this skill:

1. **Truthfulness (supreme rule).** Every statement in every document must be true
   and traceable to Bobby's master CV and real record. Tailoring means re-weighting,
   re-ordering, and re-wording TRUE facts to fit a role. It never means inventing
   experience, titles, employers, dates, skills, tools, certifications, metrics, or
   keywords Bobby cannot substantiate. No fabrication, no misrepresentation, no
   keyword-stuffing for capabilities he lacks. If a role asks for something Bobby
   does not have, leave it out; do not manufacture it. When in doubt about whether a
   claim is supportable, flag it to Bobby rather than including it.
2. **Financial framing** (Section 1).

Everything else is guidance Bobby applies selectively by role. When guidance conflicts
with either non-negotiable, the non-negotiable wins.

## 1. Financial framing — HARD RULES (never violate)

Three figures recur. Each has exactly one correct framing. These are framing
rules, not suppression rules: the point is to say what each number *is*, so a
reader never misreads scope.

**$18M — DDO stream**
- Correct: "$18M capex for the DDO stream I led."
- This is an OWNED delivery figure — the capital expenditure for the stream Bobby
  personally led.
- NEVER imply it is a Westpac group-wide budget. That over-attributes scope upward
  and is inaccurate. The phrase "capex for the stream I led" forecloses the misread;
  prefer it over the looser "stream budget".

**~$7M — Anti-Hawking stream**
- Correct: "~$7M stream budget I owned end to end."
- Also an owned figure, on the same footing as the DDO capex.

**~$1B incremental asset over 5 years — Bank of Queensland (Asset Finance)**
- This is a BUSINESS / PORTFOLIO-SCALE figure — the size of the growth over 5 years, Bobby identified can be delivered through implementing the strategy. $1B is the ceiling of the opportunity
- It is a 5 year growth opportunity identification amount.
- is NOT a personal delivery claim ("I delivered $1B" is wrong).

**~$700M+ footing — Bank of Queensland (Asset Finance)**  
- This is a BUSINESS / PORTFOLIO-SCALE figure — the size of growth opportunity Bobby has identified, that can be delivered through implementing the strategy
- It is NOT a personal delivery claim ("I delivered $700M+" is wrong).

**Context and General rule:**  

- Bobby has worked across multiple functional domains Strategy - Transformation Delivery - Procurement - Business Development / Sales - Corporate Finance as outlined in the CV
- Bobby has a well rounded skill set around hard (problem solving, numeric analysis, project management, etc), soft (communication, leadership, influencing etc)
Professional Summary to outline the experiences based on which I am pitching for this role. Cover letter to capture this pitch in sync
- All experiences are shown chronologically, highlighting the ones to strengthen my candidacy,
- Retain the recommendations
- Produce CV and cover letter in docx
- Relevant sections will be highlighted in bold 
- Professional summary keep owned-budget figures (DDO, Anti-Hawking) visually and
rhetorically separate from any market/portfolio-scale figure (BoQ). Owned numbers
carry weight precisely because they are cleanly Bobby's; do not dilute them.

## 6. Voice & style

The writing must read like Bobby, not like a sales pitch. The binding style guide is
`references/bobby-style.md`, derived from Bobby's own CV prose — read it before
drafting any document and match its cadence, vocabulary, and understatement. The
rules below are the headline points; the reference file holds the full fingerprint:

- **No aggressive-sales phrasing.** Avoid lines that pitch hard or sound like a
  closer. Banned register includes things like "this is exactly what I do," "I am
  the perfect fit," "look no further." State capability through evidence and let the
  record carry it; do not assert eagerness or fit in salesy terms.
- **Sparing use of the dash.** Do not lean on "-" (hyphen/dash) as a default
  connective in sentence structure. Prefer full sentences, commas, or a clean
  restructure. An occasional dash is fine; a paragraph peppered with them is not.
  This applies to em-dashes and en-dashes too.
- **Understated and specific over effusive.** Bobby's voice is measured: concrete
  facts and outcomes, not adjectives about himself.
- **Cover letters especially:** specific to the role and company, evidence-led,
  no boilerplate, no hard-sell closes.

The style reference is captured. If Bobby later refines his writing, update
`references/bobby-style.md` and keep treating it as the single source of voice so
CVs, cover letters, and his master document stay in sync.

## 7. Consistency audit (run on request, or during the weekly sweep)

When asked to audit the application folder, sweep every per-role file and flag:
- Any claim not traceable to the master CV or Bobby's real record — possible
  misrepresentation. Flag immediately; this is the highest-priority check.
- Any $18M reference not framed as "capex for the DDO stream I led."
- Any ~$7M reference not framed as an owned end-to-end stream budget.
- Any ~$1B BoQ figure that is unlabelled, framed as a delivery claim, or sitting
  adjacent to the owned-budget figures.
- Any core-competencies list longer than ~10 items.
- Any surviving reading-view toggle preamble.
- Any aggressive-sales phrasing in a cover letter (hard-sell closes, "perfect fit"
  register, "this is exactly what I do" type lines).
- Overuse of the dash as sentence connective.
- Generic/boilerplate cover-letter passages not tailored to the role.

Report findings as a per-file list with the specific line to fix. Do not edit
files in the audit pass — surface, then let Bobby route the fixes.

## 8. Human gates

- **Design gate:** before writing, propose the angle (which wins lead, which story,
  what's cut) and wait for Bobby's go-ahead.
- **Outcome gate:** after drafting, Bobby reviews and makes the acceptance call
  before anything is saved as a final role file or logged to the tracker.

Bobby is the router and decision-maker. When a call is genuinely two-sided, present
pros/cons rather than deciding alone.

## 9. Application workflow (the Cowork loop)

The outcome of the loop is a reviewed, ready-to-submit application pack per matched
role. It is NOT autonomous submission. Bobby applies, or approves each submit. The
loop:

1. **Source matches.** Two inputs: (a) job alerts in Bobby's email via the Gmail
   connector, and (b) roles found on the web that match his profile. For each, assess
   genuine fit against his master CV first; skip weak matches rather than forcing a
   tailor. Surface borderline ones to Bobby with a one-line fit read.
2. **Tailor for relevance.** Build a role-specific CV that strengthens alignment with
   the job description by surfacing the most relevant achievements and experience and
   de-emphasising the rest. Reweight and reorder TRUE content only (see the
   Truthfulness rule at the top — this step is where the temptation to overreach
   lives, so hold the line hard).
3. **Optimise for ATS and human readers, honestly.**
   - *ATS:* mirror the posting's real language and named competencies where Bobby
     genuinely has them; use standard section headings; avoid graphics or complex
     tables that parsers mishandle; place the role's key terms in real context, never
     stuffed. There is no universal ATS score to "max out" — alignment to the
     posting's actual requirements, using true facts, is the only real lever.
   - *Human:* lead with the strongest relevant evidence, make the first third of the
     CV carry the match, and keep Bobby's measured voice (Section 6).
4. **Assemble the pack.** Tailored CV + tailored cover letter, named per Section 5,
   plus a short fit note: why this is a match, which achievements were surfaced, and
   any gaps Bobby should know about before applying.
5. **Present for Bobby's decision (the gate).** Bobby reviews the pack and makes the
   apply/submit call. Cowork does not submit autonomously. If Bobby asks it to fill a
   portal, it works under his supervision and STOPS before final submit for his
   explicit confirmation.
6. **Log it.** Record the application in the tracker (role, company, date applied,
   stage, referral contact, next action).
