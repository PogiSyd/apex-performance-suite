# Job Search Operating Manual
 
This skill is the canonical reference for Bobby's job search. It supersedes the
earlier `SKILL.md` and `SKILL ADDENDUM.md`; both are folded in here.
 
Two things are non-negotiable and override everything else in this skill:
 
1. **Truthfulness (supreme rule).** Every statement in every document must be true
   and traceable to Bobby's master CV and real record. Tailoring means re-weighting,
   re-ordering, and re-wording TRUE facts to fit a role. It never means inventing
   experience, titles, employers, dates, skills, tools, certifications, metrics, or
   keywords Bobby cannot substantiate. No fabrication, no misrepresentation, no
   keyword-stuffing for capabilities he lacks. If a role asks for something Bobby
   does not have, leave it out; do not manufacture it. When in doubt about whether a
   claim is supportable, flag it to Bobby rather than including it.
2. **Financial framing** (Section 1).
Everything else is guidance Bobby applies selectively by role. When guidance conflicts
with either non-negotiable, the non-negotiable wins.
 
## 1. Financial framing — HARD RULES (never violate)
 
Four figures recur. Each has exactly one correct framing. These are framing rules,
not suppression rules: the point is to say what each number *is*, so a reader never
misreads scope.
 
**$18M — DDO stream (OWNED)**
 
- Correct: "$18M capex for the DDO stream I led."
- This is an OWNED delivery figure, the capital expenditure for the stream Bobby
  personally led.
- NEVER imply it is a Westpac group-wide budget. That over-attributes scope upward
  and is inaccurate. The phrase "capex for the stream I led" forecloses the misread;
  prefer it over the looser "stream budget".
**circa $7M — Anti-Hawking stream (OWNED)**
 
- Correct: "circa $7M stream budget I owned end to end."
- Also an owned figure, on the same footing as the DDO capex.
**circa $1B incremental asset over 5 years — Bank of Queensland, Asset Finance (OPPORTUNITY SCALE)**
 
- This is a business / portfolio-scale figure. It is the growth Bobby identified as
  deliverable over five years through implementing the strategy. $1B is the ceiling
  of the opportunity, not a booked result.
- It is an opportunity identification, not a personal delivery claim. "I delivered
  $1B" is wrong.
- Where used, label it explicitly as an identified five-year growth opportunity.
**$700M+ footing — Bank of Queensland, Asset Finance (OPPORTUNITY SCALE)**
 
- Same class as above. It is the size of the growth opportunity Bobby identified as
  deliverable through implementing the strategy.
- It is NOT a personal delivery claim. "I delivered $700M+" is wrong.
**The separation rule.** Keep owned figures (DDO, Anti-Hawking) visually and
rhetorically separate from any opportunity or portfolio-scale figure (BoQ). This
applies with particular force in the professional summary, where the numbers sit
closest together. Owned numbers carry weight precisely because they are cleanly
Bobby's; do not dilute them by parking a $1B next to them.
 
**Context.** Bobby has worked across multiple functional domains, Strategy,
Transformation Delivery, Procurement, Business Development and Sales, and Corporate
Finance, as set out in the master CV. His skill set spans the hard (problem solving,
numeric analysis, project management) and the soft (communication, leadership,
influencing). Draw on whichever domain the role calls for.

## 5. Per-role output conventions
 
- **Professional summary** outlines the experiences on which Bobby is pitching for
  that specific role. The **cover letter carries the same pitch, in sync**. If the
  summary and the letter tell different stories, one of them is wrong.
- **Experience is shown chronologically**, with the roles that strengthen candidacy
  for this posting highlighted rather than reordered out of sequence.
- **Retain the recommendations.** They do the praising so Bobby's own prose does not
  have to.
- **Relevant sections are bolded** so a skimming reader lands on the match.
- **Produce the CV and cover letter in docx.** HTML versions are derived where a web
  or portal format is needed (see §10 for the build pipeline).
- Each role gets its own tailored CV and cover-letter pair, using Bobby's naming
  convention, for example `cv-<company>-v<N>.docx`.
- Black fonts on formal or conservative roles: banking, government, insurance.
- Honest-bridge style on any career gaps. Explain, do not paper over.
- Cover letters are specific to the role and company. No generic boilerplate.
- The live CV lives as `index.html` at pogisyd.github.io with the Track Record and
  AI Method toggle. Role-tailored versions are derived from it.
## 6. Voice and style
 
The writing must read like Bobby, not like a sales pitch. The binding style guide is
`bobby-style.md`, derived from Bobby's own CV prose. Read it before drafting any
document and match its cadence, vocabulary, and understatement. The rules below are
the headline points; the reference file holds the full fingerprint:
 
- **No aggressive-sales phrasing.** Avoid lines that pitch hard or sound like a
  closer. Banned register includes "this is exactly what I do," "I am the perfect
  fit," "look no further." State capability through evidence and let the record
  carry it. Do not assert eagerness or fit in salesy terms.
- **Sparing use of the dash.** Do not lean on the dash as a default connective.
  Prefer full sentences, commas, or a clean restructure. An occasional dash is fine;
  a paragraph peppered with them is not. This applies to em-dashes and en-dashes too.
- **Understated and specific over effusive.** Bobby's voice is measured: concrete
  facts and outcomes, not adjectives about himself.
- **Cover letters especially:** specific to the role and company, evidence-led, no
  boilerplate, no hard-sell closes.
If Bobby later refines his writing, update `bobby-style.md` and keep treating it as
the single source of voice so CVs, cover letters, and his master document stay in
sync.
 
## 7. Consistency audit (run on request, or during the weekly sweep)
 
When asked to audit the application folder, sweep every per-role file and flag:
 
- Any claim not traceable to the master CV or Bobby's real record, as possible
  misrepresentation. Flag immediately; this is the highest-priority check.
- Any $18M reference not framed as "capex for the DDO stream I led."
- Any circa $7M reference not framed as an owned end-to-end stream budget.
- Any BoQ figure ($1B or $700M+) that is unlabelled, framed as a delivery claim, or
  sitting adjacent to the owned-budget figures.
- Any professional summary and cover letter that pitch different stories.
- Any file where experience is not in chronological order, or where the
  recommendations have been dropped.
- Any core-competencies list longer than about 10 items.
- Any surviving reading-view toggle preamble.
- Any aggressive-sales phrasing in a cover letter: hard-sell closes, "perfect fit"
  register, "this is exactly what I do" lines.
- Overuse of the dash as sentence connective.
- Generic or boilerplate cover-letter passages not tailored to the role.
- Any AI keyword ("AI governance", "AI delivery", "prompt engineering") used where
  Bobby's record does not support it.
Report findings as a per-file list with the specific line to fix. Do not edit files
in the audit pass. Surface, then let Bobby route the fixes.
 
## 8. Human gates
 
- **Design gate:** before writing, propose the angle (which wins lead, which story,
  what is cut) and wait for Bobby's go-ahead.
- **Outcome gate:** after drafting, Bobby reviews and makes the acceptance call
  before anything is saved as a final role file or logged to the tracker.
Bobby is the router and decision-maker. When a call is genuinely two-sided, present
pros and cons rather than deciding alone.
 
## 9. Application workflow (the Cowork loop)
 
The outcome of the loop is a reviewed, ready-to-submit application pack per matched
role. It is NOT autonomous submission. Bobby applies, or approves each submit. The
loop:
 
1. **Source matches.** Inputs are the morning sweep (§12), job alerts in Bobby's
   email, and roles found on the web. For each, assess genuine fit against the master
   CV first; skip weak matches rather than forcing a tailor. Surface borderline ones
   with a one-line fit read.
2. **Tailor for relevance.** Build a role-specific CV that strengthens alignment with
   the job description by surfacing the most relevant achievements and experience and
   de-emphasising the rest. Reweight and reword TRUE content only. This step is where
   the temptation to overreach lives, so hold the line hard.
3. **Optimise for ATS and human readers, honestly.**
   - *ATS:* mirror the posting's real language and named competencies where Bobby
     genuinely has them; use standard section headings; avoid graphics or complex
     tables that parsers mishandle; place the role's key terms in real context, never
     stuffed. There is no universal ATS score to max out. Alignment to the posting's
     actual requirements, using true facts, is the only real lever.
   - *Human:* lead with the strongest relevant evidence, make the first third of the
     CV carry the match, and keep Bobby's measured voice (§6).
4. **Assemble the pack.** Tailored CV and tailored cover letter in docx, named per
   §5, plus a short fit note: why this is a match, which achievements were surfaced,
   and any gaps Bobby should know about before applying.
5. **Present for Bobby's decision (the gate).** Bobby reviews the pack and makes the
   apply or submit call. Cowork does not submit autonomously. If Bobby asks it to
   fill a portal, it works under his supervision and STOPS before final submit for
   his explicit confirmation.
6. **Log it.** Record the application in `tracker.md`: role, company, date applied,
   stage, referral contact, next action.
## 10. Token economics (applies to every task and every chat in this project)
 
Tokens are the working budget. The rules below are binding for all work here,
including research, drafting, document production, and the job sweep.
 
**Markdown is the working format. HTML and docx are publishing steps.**
 
- All drafting, iteration, review, and Bobby's feedback cycles happen in markdown.
  Markdown is cheap to write, cheap to diff, and cheap to correct.
- Never iterate inside HTML or docx. Rewriting a styled document to change a
  sentence costs many times what the sentence is worth.
- Convert to the delivery format only once the markdown is accepted at the outcome
  gate (§8).
**Produce the output once, then edit surgically.**
 
- Generate the HTML or docx a single time from the approved markdown.
- Every change after that is a targeted edit to the existing file: replace the exact
  string, the exact paragraph, the exact block. Never regenerate the whole document
  to change part of it.
- If a change is so broad that surgical edits stop making sense, go back to the
  markdown, revise there, and regenerate once.
- The same rule governs this file and every other project doc.
**Read once.**
 
- Each source (master CV, `bobby-style.md`, Job Criteria, `tracker.md`, a job
  description) is read at most once per session. Work from what was read.
- Do not re-read a file after editing it to confirm the edit landed.
- Stage local files once per session, not per task.
**Do not echo.**
 
- Never paste a full CV, job description, or document back into the chat. Quote only
  the specific lines under discussion.
- Report deltas, not recaps. Bobby has been following along.
**Search discipline.**
 
- Before any web search, check whether the project already answers it: master CV,
  Job Criteria, tracker, prior packs. Most questions about Bobby are answered inside
  the project.
- Search for facts that genuinely change the output: a live posting, a company's
  current structure, a salary benchmark. Do not search to decorate.
- Batch related queries into one pass. Cap results and read snippets first. Fetch a
  full page only when the snippet cannot carry the point, and never fetch the same
  URL twice.
- For open-ended or broad research, route to Gemini (§13) rather than burning the
  session's context on exploratory browsing.
**Scope discipline.**
 
- One role per pass. Do not preload several job descriptions into one session.
- Fit-gate before tailoring. Assess fit from the posting summary; only pull the full
  description for roles that survive the gate.
- Bundle clarifying questions into one round rather than asking serially.
## 11. Fresh-chat transition (prompting Bobby to move)
 
Long chats carry their whole history into every turn. Past a point, the cheapest
improvement available is a new chat.
 
**Persist before proposing.** Durable state lives in the project, never in chat
memory: the application pack, `tracker.md`, decisions locked, and any updated skill
doc. Write these first, then propose the switch. A handover should never be the only
copy of anything.
 
**Propose a fresh chat when any of these is true:**
 
- A complete application pack has been delivered and accepted.
- Two or more roles have been worked in the same session.
- A long stretch of web research or document conversion has just finished.
- The task type is changing, for example from tailoring a CV to maintaining these
  skills, or from the morning sweep to drafting.
- Responses are slowing, or Bobby says the chat feels heavy.
**How to propose it.** State it plainly in one line, then give Bobby a handover block
he can paste into the new chat. Do not lecture about context windows.
 
```
Recommend picking this up in a fresh chat. Handover to paste:
 
HANDOVER — Job Search
Done this session: <one line per completed item>
Files written: <paths in the project or repo>
Decisions locked: <framing calls, angle choices Bobby approved>
Open questions: <anything awaiting Bobby>
Next action: <the single next step>
Read first: SKILL.md, bobby-style.md, master CV
```
 
Bobby makes the call. If he wants to continue in the same chat, continue without
raising it again for that stretch of work.
 
## 12. Morning job sweep (run when prompted)
 
**Trigger.** Bobby says "morning sweep", "check jobs", or similar. This never runs
autonomously and never applies to anything.
 
**Sources, in order:**
 
1. **Gmail.** Job alert emails from the last 24 hours: Seek, LinkedIn, Indeed,
   recruiter and agency alerts. Search the inbox by sender and date rather than
   reading whole threads.
2. **Indeed.** Search directly through the Indeed connector using the Job Criteria
   terms.
3. **Other connected boards.** ZipRecruiter and any other connected job source.
4. **Anything else Bobby names.** Where a board is not connected, route the search
   through Gemini (§13) rather than browsing it turn by turn.
**Filter.** The binding filter is the `Job Criteria` doc in the project. In short:
Program Manager, Project Manager, Delivery Lead, or Project Director; in financial
services, banking, fintech, insurance, or wealth management; Sydney; contract at
circa $1,250 per day or above, or permanent at circa $220k or above. Read the
criteria doc for the exact boolean before running the sweep.
 
**De-duplicate** against `tracker.md`. Do not re-surface roles already logged,
applied to, or previously dismissed.
 
**Output.** One compact markdown table, capped at about ten rows, ordered by fit:
 
| Role | Company | Source | Rate or salary | Posted | Fit | Note |
 
Fit is Strong, Possible, or Skip, with a note of no more than one line saying why.
Include the link. Nothing else: no tailoring, no drafting, no analysis of roles Bobby
has not picked.
 
**Then stop.** Bobby picks. Only for the picked roles does the loop pull the full job
description and move into §9. Given the token rules in §10, a sweep and a tailoring
pass are usually better run as separate chats (§11).
 
**Optional automation.** A scheduled task can post the sweep each morning, but the
same rules hold: it surfaces a shortlist and stops. It never tailors, never applies,
and never submits.
 
## 13. Routing web search through Gemini
 
Where a search is broad, exploratory, or likely to require reading several pages,
Bobby runs it in Gemini and pastes the result back. This keeps this session's context
for the work that needs Bobby's record.
 
**Route to Gemini when:**
 
- Researching a company, its structure, leadership, or recent news for a cover letter
  or interview.
- Benchmarking day rates or salaries for a role type in the Sydney market.
- Scanning job boards that are not connected here.
- Any question that would need more than about three pages read to answer.
**Do not route** a single fact that one lookup settles, or anything already answered
by the project docs.
 
**How it works.** Claude does not browse for these. It writes one self-contained
prompt in a code block for Bobby to paste into Gemini, and it specifies the return
format so the paste-back is compact.
 
```
Search the web and answer the question below.
 
QUESTION
<the specific question>
 
CONSTRAINTS
- Australian sources and Sydney market unless stated otherwise.
- Current information only. Note the date of anything time-sensitive.
 
RETURN FORMAT
- A markdown table with these columns: <columns>.
- No preamble, no summary paragraph, no commentary.
- One row per finding, maximum <N> rows.
- Include the source URL and publication date in the final column.
```
 
**On the way back.**
 
- Bobby pastes the table. Claude treats it as source data and cites it where it is
  used.
- Anything from Gemini that would end up in a CV or cover letter is checked against
  Bobby's own record first. The truthfulness rule in the header governs regardless of
  where a fact came from.
- If the returned data is thin, tighten the prompt and ask once more rather than
  falling back to browsing here.
